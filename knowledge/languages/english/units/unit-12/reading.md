## Текст 1: The History of Cinema (История кинематографа)

The history of cinema goes back to the end of the 19th century. The first films were very short — usually less than one minute. The Lumière brothers made their famous short film *"Workers Leaving the Factory"* in 1895. However, cinema was not really a form of art yet; it was just a technical wonder.

In the 1920s films changed completely. Directors started to experiment with editing, close-ups and special effects. Silent movies with black-and-white pictures became very popular, and actors like Charlie Chaplin became world-famous. Chaplin's films are still watched today because his comedy is timeless.

In 1927, the first film with sound, *"The Jazz Singer"*, appeared. Soon after, colour films arrived. Movies became longer, more complex and more expensive to make. Hollywood grew into the biggest film industry in the world.

After the Second World War, international cinema developed quickly. Italian, French and later Asian directors created new styles and ideas. What is more, the invention of television, and later the internet, changed the way we watch films. Today millions of people watch movies on small screens — on their phones and computers.

Technology continues to move fast. Virtual reality, artificial intelligence and new 3D formats are changing cinema again. But one thing never changes: people love a good story. In fact, cinema is often called the most popular art form of the 20th century.

3. The first film with sound, *"The Jazz Singer"*, appeared in 1927.
4. They changed the way we watch films — today many people watch movies on small screens (phones, computers).
5. Because people love a good story, and technology keeps cinema popular.

## Текст 2: A Famous Museum (Британский музей)

The British Museum in London is one of the most famous museums in the world. It was founded in 1753 and opened its doors to the public in 1759. The museum has more than eight million objects, and its collection never stops growing.

Walking through the museum is like travelling around the world. Visitors can see the Rosetta Stone, an ancient Egyptian treasure that helped scientists read hieroglyphs. They can also admire the Elgin Marbles, sculptures which were brought from Greece over two hundred years ago. The collection of mummies attracts children and adults alike, and the room with the clockwork models is always full of families.

About six million people visit the British Museum every year, and entry is completely free. Interesting fact: it was the first national museum in the world that everyone could visit free of charge. Sometimes visitors wait in a long queue, but the experience is worth it.

Special exhibitions are organised regularly. Some of them are so popular that tickets must be booked weeks in advance. The museum also runs educational programmes for schools and hosts evening lectures for everyone who wants to know more about art and history.

The British Museum shows that culture belongs to everyone. It is not just a beautiful building; it is a bridge between past and present, between different countries and cultures.

## Текст 3: A Traditional Celebration (Хэллоуин)

Halloween, which is celebrated on the 31st of October, is one of the oldest and most famous festivals in English-speaking countries. Its roots go back to ancient Celtic traditions. Over the centuries the festival changed several times, and today it is a mixture of old customs and modern fun.

Children love Halloween because of the tradition of "trick or treat". They dress up in costumes — as witches, ghosts, superheroes or characters from their favourite films — and go from door to door asking for sweets. Most adults join the game with pleasure, which makes the evening friendly and funny.

People also decorate their houses. The most well-known symbol of Halloween is the carved pumpkin, called a "jack-o'-lantern". Families put glowing lanterns near their doors. Parties and parades are held in many cities, and in the evening the sky is bright with fireworks.

Some customs are older than others. For example, bonfires were lit by Celts to welcome the winter and to remember ancestors. Modern Halloween is lighter and more playful than its ancient form, but the idea is still the same: people come together, share stories and have a good time.

Today Halloween is celebrated not only in the USA, Great Britain and Canada, but in many other countries as well. Critics say that the festival is becoming too commercial. However, for millions of people it remains a joyful tradition that brings neighbours and families closer.
